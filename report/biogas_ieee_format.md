\begin{abstract}
This study was carried out in response to the growing interest on household biogas digesters in Latin America, particularly in rural Andean communities. The aim was to compare the fixed dome and plastic tubular digester in terms of biogas production, cost and environmental impact, using the life cycle assessment methodology. Design and operational parameters, construction materials and implementation costs were based on our previous research and literature results for plastic tubular and fixed dome digesters, respectively. According to this analysis, the main advantage of the plastic tubular digester was its ease of implementation and handling, and lower investment cost compared to the fixed dome digester, which appeared to be more environmentally friendly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Household biogas plants have been used for several decades in rural areas of Asian countries like China and India \cite{b1}. The most widely used household systems are the Chinese fixed dome digester, the Indian floating drum digester and the Taiwanese plastic tubular digester \cite{b5}. The most appropriate design depends on weather conditions and socio-economic context.

In Latin America, household digesters have been spreading since the 1980s in rural areas of tropical countries like Colombia and Costa Rica and hilly regions of Peru and Bolivia \cite{b12}. Basic objectives of the biogas program were: (i) to generate energy for cooking and lighting, satisfying household needs; (ii) to avoid deforestation and its effect on soil erosion; (iii) to reduce environmental pollution; and (iv) to improve household sanitation. 

During the last decade, there has been a growing interest in implementing plastic tubular digesters in rural Andean communities; because of its low-cost, and ease of implementation and handling. The aim of this research was to compare the performance, cost and potential environmental impact of fixed dome and plastic tubular digesters implemented at high altitude.

\section{Material and Methods}

\subsection{Household Digesters Performance}
The study considered two types of household digesters adapted to Andean conditions: 1) fixed dome - Chinese model and 2) plastic tubular - Taiwanese model. Chinese fixed dome digesters consist of a cylindrical chamber, a feedstock inlet and a digestate outlet, which also serves as a compensation tank. In plastic tubular digesters, wastewater flows through a tubular polyethylene or PVC bag (the reactor) from the inlet to the outlet, while biogas is collected by means of a gas pipe connected to a reservoir.

\subsection{Economic Analysis}
The economic analysis was based on the digesters capital cost, which accounts for the major cost of household biogas plants. Design parameters of digesters implemented in Peru were adopted for the Chinese fixed dome and plastic tubular digesters.

\subsection{Life Cycle Assessment}
Life cycle assessment (LCA) is a methodological tool for the evaluation of environmental aspects and potential impacts through the whole life of a product, process or service. The goal of this LCA model was to compare the environmental impact of both household digesters implemented at high altitude. The functional unit was defined as the production of 1 m$^3$ of biogas.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Household Digesters Performance at High Altitude}
Literature results show how the biogas production rate may range between 0.03 and 0.7 m$^3_\text{biogas}$ m$^{-3}_\text{digester}$ d$^{-1}$, depending on the feedstock and operational parameters. Plastic tubular digesters were adapted to Andean conditions by providing digesters insulation and substituting a simple roof used in Tropical regions by a greenhouse. In this manner, the liquor temperature remained fairly constant at around 20 \textdegree C.

\subsection{Economic Analysis}
The economic assessment was based on digesters capital cost, \$1963 for the fixed dome model and \$1729 for the plastic tubular model. Bricks and cement accounted for 60\% of the total cost in the fixed dome digester. On the other hand, the geomembrane was the most expensive material in the plastic tubular digester (around 70\% of the total cost). Manpower was about 30\% and 7\% of total cost in the fixed dome and plastic tubular digester, respectively.

\subsection{Life Cycle Assessment}
The fixed dome model showed the lowest impact in all categories, appearing as the best alternative from an environmental point of view. The highest impact of the tubular digester was attributed to the plastic employed for the greenhouse and biogas reservoir construction, followed by the geomembrane. In the fixed dome digester, the main impact was attributed to cement ($\approx$50\% in all categories) and bricks ($\approx$30\% in all categories) used for the dome construction.

\section{Conclusions}
This study compared two household digester models implemented at high altitude. Over a lifespan of 20 years, the capital cost was 12\% lower for the plastic tubular digester compared to the fixed dome digester. The main advantage of the tubular digester was its ease of implementation and handling, and lower investment cost compared to the fixed dome digester, which appeared to be more environmentally friendly.
